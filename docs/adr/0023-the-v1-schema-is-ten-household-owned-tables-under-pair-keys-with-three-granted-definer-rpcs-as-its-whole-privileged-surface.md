# The v1 schema is ten Household-owned tables under pair keys, with three granted definer RPCs as its whole privileged surface

> **Amends [ADR-0016](0016-buy-takes-the-whole-basket-and-claims-rows-on-the-servers-clock.md):** the Buy RPC is `public.buy_basket`, not `private.buy_basket` — PostgREST only reaches functions in an exposed schema, so a `private` RPC is unreachable from a phone. Its clock, guard, session token and refusal are unchanged.
> **Amends [ADR-0017](0017-purchase-lines-reference-rows-and-every-delete-cascades.md):** "a Line dies with its Item, not with its List Item". A Line now *names* the List Item it claimed, so **deleting a List erases the Lines that named its Rows**, and a Purchase whose every Line named a Row of that List is erased by ADR-0017's own trigger. History is not untouched by a List delete.
> **Amends [ADR-0019](0019-a-sign-up-seeds-its-household-by-trigger.md):** the function is `private.handle_new_user`, and the definer rule ("out of the exposed API schemas, revoked from `anon` and `authenticated`") has one exception, written below.
> **Amends [ADR-0022](0022-hygiene-verbs-live-on-the-entity-s-own-surface-and-a-cascade-confirmation-names-only-what-the-household-can-see.md):** "no new RPC" and "zero new grants" describe the hygiene *verbs*, verified true for renames, Tags, Deletes and Location edits. Join, Preview and Buy are three RPCs, and the two rosters are views.

[ADR-0009](0009-households-own-everything.md) said every noun hangs off a Household; [ticket #3](https://github.com/justintaddei/kinetic-lists/issues/3) is the shape that saying becomes SQL. **Ten tables in `public`, every one of them Row-Level-Secured, no `anon` policy anywhere, and one denormalized `household_id` on every owned row** — Locations, List Items, Purchase Lines and Arrivals all carry their Household directly rather than joining to find it, because a policy is then one column and a cascade has one thing to index. Ids are `uuid` defaulting to `extensions.uuidv7()`: sortable, unguessable, mintable client-side, and free of the sequences whose grants on the demo table put `anon` on a `bigint` counter (#16's sequence-grant warning, ended by never having a sequence). `household_invites.token` is a `uuid` defaulting to `gen_random_uuid()`, which is the primary key — the link is a row's identity, not a value attached to one.

**Pair-validity is a composite key, not a trigger.** Every pair a write names — a Row's List and Household, a Line's Row and Item, a Purchase's Store and Household, an Arrival's Location and Household, a Location's Store and Household — is a **composite foreign key into a composite unique key** (`unique (household_id, "id")` on every table a pair points into), so a row that names a Store of another Household, a Location of another Store, or a Row of another Household **cannot exist**: the pair is enforced by the same index that makes the write's tenancy check cheap. [ADR-0017](0017-purchase-lines-reference-rows-and-every-delete-cascades.md)'s "the guard runs inside the write, where a client cannot talk past it" is now a fact of the schema rather than a promise of the RPC. Every one of them is `ON DELETE CASCADE`, per [ADR-0017](0017-purchase-lines-reference-rows-and-every-delete-cascades.md) and Justin's ruling on a Location's pair: **a Location delete erases the Purchases and Arrivals that named it, in silence** — the load-bearing rule behind [ADR-0022](0022-hygiene-verbs-live-on-the-entity-s-own-surface-and-a-cascade-confirmation-names-only-what-the-household-can-see.md)'s confirmations naming only live rows. The one pair that crosses two tables no key connects — a Store Tag's Item and Store — is the schema's single pair **trigger**, `before insert or update of item_id, store_id`, raising `23514`; pair-validity by index covers pairs a key can name, and RLS cannot read a Store Tag's Item, so a tag gets the trigger.

**Tenancy is read by a `security definer` helper, `private.is_member(household_id)`, and every policy on every table is `(select private.is_member(household_id))` (plus verb and column) — with one exception a definer cannot help: `household_members`' own policy is `auth.uid() = user_id`.** A policy on `household_members` that read `household_members` would be a recursive policy (Postgres `42P17`): RLS would need to see a row to decide whether to show it. A definer reading it past RLS breaks that loop, and `private` is not in `[api] schemas = ["public", "graphql_public"]`, so the helper has no HTTP surface at all. The same shape serves the two writes a Member's policy cannot judge alone: a Buy naming a Location of a foreign Store, and a Join reading the token's Household before the caller has any right to see it.

**The definer set is three granted functions in `public` — that is the whole privileged surface — and five ungranted functions in `private`.** Granted: `buy_basket(store_id, location_id, sold_at, session_token)`, `join_household(token)`, `preview_invitation(token)`, each `grant execute ... to authenticated`, the two Join functions `revoke`d from `anon` and `public`, `preview_invitation` granted to `anon` (a signed-out Visitor is exactly who reads it, [ADR-0014](0014-household-membership-is-granted-by-an-invitation-token.md)). Ungranted: `is_member`, `handle_new_user`, and the three erasers. PostgREST exposes only functions in an exposed schema, and only to roles the function grants, so [ADR-0019](0019-a-sign-up-seeds-its-household-by-trigger.md)'s "out of the exposed API schemas, revoked from `anon` and `authenticated`" **cannot** be obeyed by an RPC the phone calls: for app RPCs the rule flips to *in `public`, granted to `authenticated`, revoked from `anon` and `public`*; for helpers and triggers it stands exactly as written. Lint `0029` ("grant `anon`/`authenticated` execute on a `security definer` function") fires on `preview_invitation` and, with `0028`, on all three RPCs — **that grant is the app's contract, not an accident**: intentional, recorded in `docs/agents/advisors.md`, and suppressed per object. An `is_member` minted as a public definer RPC is the warning worth heeding — a general read of the Membership table, callable by anyone with a JWT, serving nothing; the pair-validity keys make every such check a join. The register the suppressions live in is [`docs/agents/advisors.md`](../agents/advisors.md), read before any ticket touches `supabase/`.

**Everything else is a policy verb.** `SELECT · INSERT · UPDATE · DELETE` per table, `to authenticated`, tenancy by pair key: Store rename, List rename, Household rename, Display Name, Item edits, List Item Status moves (`needed ↔ in_basket` — `stocked_at` and `stocked` are never written from the browser, [ADR-0016](0016-buy-takes-the-whole-basket-and-claims-rows-on-the-servers-clock.md)), minted Invitations, Store Tags, Locations, Arrivals (`with check (user_id = auth.uid() and is_member(household_id))` — the phone records its own Arrival, [ADR-0005](0005-arrival-is-client-side-and-only-suggests.md)). Postgres gives a policy **no column list** — `CREATE POLICY ... FOR UPDATE (name)` is not a thing — so a verb's floor is the values a `with check` permits, and the schema states its limits as values: a Row's insert and update policies require `status in ('needed', 'in_basket')`, which is a stronger sentence than naming columns, because **the app has no way to write `stocked` at all**. Where a value check cannot reach, a trigger does: `before update of id, household_id, store_id, user_id, list_id, item_id` raises on every owned table, so **a row's ownership keys never move** — a Location cannot be slid onto a sibling Store, a List cannot be re-homed, and a Member cannot walk their own Membership into a Household no one invited them to. That last one is the reason the erasers and this guard are in the schema and not in app code: `household_members` has an `UPDATE` policy a Member's JWT satisfies, and the pair keys are exactly what it must not be able to move. There is **no `DELETE` policy on `list_items`**: a Member un-Checks a row, they do not delete the Household's standing membership outside List hygiene, and [ADR-0022](0022-hygiene-verbs-live-on-the-entity-s-own-surface-and-a-cascade-confirmation-names-only-what-the-household-can-see.md) refused the List delete verb, so `lists` has none. **`purchases` and `purchase_lines` have zero grants** — no `select`, no `insert`, no `update`, no `delete` — and Supabase's default privileges in `public` are revoked on both. v1 has no history screen ([ADR-0016](0016-buy-takes-the-whole-basket-and-claims-rows-on-the-servers-clock.md)), and [ADR-0004](0004-purchases-are-append-only-snapshots.md)'s append-only rule is enforced by the absence of a door, with `create policy ... using (false)` written out so the absence reads as a decision. **The app writes no timestamp**: `bought_at`, `stocked_at`, `arrived_at`, `created_at`, `expires_at` and `accepted_at` all default to `now()` server-side, `sold_at` is client-supplied as a `date` and stored as one ([ADR-0016](0016-buy-takes-the-whole-basket-and-claims-rows-on-the-servers-clock.md)), and `buy_basket` takes its clock once, as `transaction_timestamp()`, the transaction's start.

**`purchases` has no `list_id`.** [ADR-0015](0015-store-tags-are-written-at-capture-and-an-arrival-selects-the-active-store.md)'s Buy is the whole In-Basket basket at the Active Store, **cross-List** — one Purchase, Lines naming Rows on any number of Lists; the List a shopped Row belonged to lives in the Row the Line names. A Purchase's trip identity is `(household_id, session_token)`, unique per Household: the replayed token returns the recorded Purchase and writes nothing ([ADR-0016](0016-buy-takes-the-whole-basket-and-claims-rows-on-the-servers-clock.md)), and the claim update — `status = 'in_basket' and (stocked_at is null or stocked_at <= transaction start)`, restricted to rows Tagged at the Store — raises for a Buy that claims nothing, so a Purchase always has ≥1 Line, and `after delete` on Lines erases a Purchase whose last Line was cascaded ([ADR-0017](0017-purchase-lines-reference-rows-and-every-delete-cascades.md)).

**The Kinetic View is not a view: it is `list_items` for one Household in one Status, and the index that serves it is `(household_id, status)`.** The two **rosters** [ADR-0022](0022-hygiene-verbs-live-on-the-entity-s-own-surface-and-a-cascade-confirmation-names-only-what-the-household-can-see.md) invented — `Walmart · 2 Locations` and `Milk · 3 Lists · Walmart, Big Y` — are the schema's two views, `roster_stores` and `roster_items`, built **`with (security_invoker = on)`** in `public` and granted `select`: the querying Member's own RLS runs inside them, so they add no privileged surface (lint `0010`, "views defined as security definer", is the reason the option is mandatory, not stylistic).

**Hygiene lives in triggers, all of them in the schema.** A sign-up seeds its Household on `after insert on auth.users` with `private.handle_new_user` — `raw_user_meta_data`'s chain, then the email's local part; no exception handler, so a raising trigger aborts the sign-up rather than leave half a Household ([ADR-0019](0019-a-sign-up-seeds-its-household-by-trigger.md)), granted `execute` to `supabase_auth_admin`, the role GoTrue inserts as. `after delete` on `household_members` erases a Household that lost its last Member (so `auth.users` deletes cascade into erased Households). A Store Tag's pair check is the trigger named above. `stores.name` and `items.name` are per-Household unique by `unique (household_id, lower(trim(name)))`: `23505` is the backstop Capture and rename read as a Merge/Make/refuse offer ([ADR-0018](0018-store-and-item-names-are-household-unique-by-index.md), [ADR-0022](0022-hygiene-verbs-live-on-the-entity-s-own-surface-and-a-cascade-confirmation-names-only-what-the-household-can-see.md)). Every cascading key carries the index [ADR-0017](0017-purchase-lines-reference-rows-and-every-delete-cascades.md) asked for, on its referencing side.

**The schema is declarative, applied through migration history, and it starts by erasing the demo table.** `supabase/schemas/*.sql` (pg-delta, `[experimental.pgdelta] enabled = true` in `supabase/config.toml`) is the source of truth; `supabase db schema declarative sync` diffs these files against the hosted database and mints a migration, never mutating in place. `supabase/schemas/lists.sql` — the demo table with its "TEMPORARY" full-access policy — is deleted, **and its migration stays in `supabase/migrations/`**, because history is history: `db pull` refuses a remote migration with no local file, so the demo table retires through a generated `drop table` migration minted from the declarative diff, and #4's CI mints it on a shadow database with `--no-apply`. The hosted baseline (`db reset --experimental`, then `db pull`) is an operator step on the hosted project, recorded with this ADR; the hosted database is never reset from a checkout. `db lint` is `plpgsql_check` over function bodies only — the lint register, the suppressions, and the hosted grant and publication procedures live in `docs/agents/advisors.md`, which every future ticket's schema work reads first. `Status` is a `text` with a `check` (`'needed'`, `'in_basket'`, `'stocked'`), not an enum: `alter type ... add value` needs a lock and a deploy, a `check` is a constraint, and the vocabulary is closed in v1.

Consequences by ticket: **#4** gets its fixture shapes (sign-up seeds a Household by trigger, and local email sign-in is what makes the chain testable), and every test needs a `uuid` id from the client. **#4** runs `db lint`, `supabase db advisors` and declarative sync's `--dry-run` as its merge gate, mints the `lists` retirement migration, and carries the grant posture on a shadow database — the advisory suppressions applied out-of-band (Supabase Studio → Advisor rules) against the hosted project, since the CLI has no in-repo suppression. **#7**'s write seams are the policy verbs above; its Capture path is a nested insert (`item` + `list_items` + `item_store_tags`), one statement, atomic by construction; and its `app/types/database.types.ts` is regenerated after the schema lands, with `supabase db gen-types`. **#8**'s seam streams `public`'s tables through Realtime; `item_store_tags` carries `replica identity full` so a tag delete streams its composite key. **#11** prototypes against `list_items`, `roster_stores` and `roster_items` and `buy_basket`'s jsonb. fog: **pair-validity by composite key is a schema fact**, so #11's "Active Store whose Location is deleted" fall and #6's "Standing Location whose Location is deleted" fall are the same null-join reading, and the Buy toast's payload is `buy_basket`'s `skipped` array.

Rejected, and why: **`bigint identity` ids** (Justin's ruling; a public sequence's grants are the trap #16 warned about, and a nested insert needs an id the phone can name before the insert); **a richer schema for the Join RPC's emptiness rule** (one `unique (user_id)`, the RPC's own read as the check); **`is_member` as a granted public RPC** (lint `0029`'s real warning: a general membership read, callable by any JWT, serving nothing); **views defined `security definer`** (lint `0010`, and a door that reads past RLS for two counts); **`RESTRICT` cascades on Locations** (Justin kept [ADR-0017](0017-purchase-lines-reference-rows-and-every-delete-cascades.md)'s cascade as written: history is disposable, and confirmation copy names live rows only); **a pair trigger for every pair** (composite keys do that work as indexes the app queries through, and a trigger per pair is 6 functions for what 11 unique keys state); **a `Status` enum** (a closed vocabulary that needs an unblockable DDL to grow, for a set of three values v1 never grows); **PostgREST aggregates** (`db-aggregates-enabled`, a role setting no declarative file can express, for counts the client does not need per-row); **batched requests and `Prefer: transaction` as a dependency** (two operations in one transaction is refused: an atomic multi-table write is a **nested insert**, which PostgREST compiles into one statement); **a `public.is_member` policy with no definer** (`42P17`, a recursive policy on `household_members`); **column lists on policies** (Postgres has none — `FOR UPDATE (name)` is not grammar, found by running the schema through `libpg_query`: a policy's floor is a `with check` over values, and the keys a Member must not move are a trigger's business); **a Buy RPC the client hands the basket to** (the server reads the Rows itself, [ADR-0016](0016-buy-takes-the-whole-basket-and-claims-rows-on-the-servers-clock.md)); **`Prefer: tx=merged` as a dependency** (every multi-table write v1 makes is one statement — a nested insert, or one RPC — so the preference is never load-bearing).
